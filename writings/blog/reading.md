Truth be told, I used to read more, years ago. It's been a while since I pick up a novel and start reading it without getting distracted or interrupted by life events. I remember it all started with Harry Potter (even if right now I try to avoid anything related to it...), Narnia, some "young adult" books and a failed attempt to go through The Lord of The Rings in Spanish... It may have been the specifc translation, but when I compared it to the English version, the latter was way easier to read for me. I managed to finish the first book but I decided not to go on at that time.

Nowadays I tend to read manga, more than anything. As in **a lot of manga**. As of writing and according to my [MyAnimeList](https://myanimelist.net/profile/mathiassm) profile, I have completed 209 series (535 volumes), and I'm currently reading 358 (which is of course an exageration, as I basically mark anything I start as "currently reading" and only some times do I actually mark it as dropped if I'm sure I'm never finishing it). May sound lame, but I'm a bit proud of my ratings having a somewhat normal distribution centered only at 5.8, as most MAL users give very positively-skewed scores.

## Some manga recomendations

Nobody asked, but why not?

From the start, regardless of who you are, what you do, what you like, where you live, what you do, ... Read [One Piece](https://myanimelist.net/manga/13) (ワンピース, _Wan Pīsu_). If you can only choose one thing to read, pick that. It's long, not yet finished, and many get intimidated by it, but I don't know a single person who doesn't love it once they start. I didn't even start reading it until 2025, after many failed attempts at the anime (dropping off at _Alabasta_ with the terrible pacing of the adaptation). Right now I'm caught up, and it's being 100% the best thing I'm have been reading overall.

#### [Fullmetal Alchemist](https://myanimelist.net/manga/25) ([鋼](-はがね)の[錬金術師](-れんきんじゅつし), _Hagane no Renkinjutsushi_)

![Fullmetal Alchemist volume 1 cover](https://cdn.myanimelist.net/images/manga/3/243675.jpg)

Maybe the most known one here. Follow the story of two brothers in a world where "Alchemy" is practiced as a science (although it works as the magic system in the story). After a traumatic event during their childhood, the are on a self-imposed mission to recover what they lost.

The main theme is (in my opinion), the question: "What is the value of a human life?". From that, many other questions arise within the grounded world of Hiromu Arakawa. Trauma, family, religion, politics, good vs evil. It may simplify some of these topics, of course, but I still think they are handled very well.

The fact that the magic system is based on knowledge and scientific training is also a plus.

Arakawa also wrote [Silver Spoon](https://myanimelist.net/manga/25096) (_Gin no Saji_), a far more chill story about an agricultural school, if you want proof of how wide her range is.

#### [Attack on Titan](https://myanimelist.net/manga/23390) ([進撃](-しんげき)の[巨人](-きょじん), _Shingeki no Kyojin_)

![Attack on Titan volume 1 cover](https://cdn.myanimelist.net/images/manga/2/37846.jpg)

More recent, and some woul say more modern. Humanity has been reduced and secluded within tall walls to flee from the titans, creatures with no intellect but a constant unrelenting drive to eat said humans. After 100 years of peace within the walls, which are taller than those titans by a long shot, a _colossal_ titan appears, looking over the wall as if it weren't 50 meters tall, kicks a hole into it, and dissapears, letting in a horde of titans. The story will follow a few later-turned cadets of the anti-titan millitary force, with the impossible goal to erradicate them, and free humanity.

It's a tricky entry, because beyond a surface of action and gore, it deals with darker themes of politics, history, fascism, genocide, wrapped in the fantasy mystery of the titans. I enjoyed the foreshadowing, the mystery, the incredible revelations every once in a while (most readers remember the exact chapter number or name where certain questions were answered). The author started the manga pretending it to be the usual action-packed fantansy non-sense, only to uncover a incresingly complicated world little by little, increasing tension and worries everywhere.

It's not perfect; in particular, certain "answers" given at the very end were _controversial_ to say the least (specially looking at the real world right now...). You don't have to agree with the answers given (the very author doesn't even seem to agree, either, from what I've read), but the fact it made people question and discuss these topics is worth a lot, I feel.

It's by Hajime Isayama, and as far as I know he hasn't really done anything else major — this is his whole thing, which honestly makes the consistency of the mystery even more impressive.

#### [Chainsaw Man](https://myanimelist.net/manga/116778) (チェンソーマン, _Chensōman_)

![Chainsaw Man volume 1 cover](https://cdn.myanimelist.net/images/manga/3/216464.jpg)

Assuming you know nothing about this, I wonder what you might imagine this entry is about. We follow Denji, a guys that has no family, no friends, no money, only his pet dog Pochita, and the incredibly dangerous job of devil hunter, under an abussive boss that only manipulating Denji as long as he's useful. Oh, and Pochita is the chainsaw devil. And because of "reasons" Denji gains the power to turn into said chainsaw devil... Only to fall into a (slightly better-paid) devil-hunting job.

It's about a guy that never went to school, never socialized, doesn't know what's normal, doesn't know what's common or expected. He, however, is trying to figure out what is his dream. What is even a dream? Should you even measure it? Someone might want the moon, but he might be happy with eating every day. Denji is not a thankless prick, or a thankful monk, he's just a guy who doesn't know better, and is constantly trying to figure out if he even wants to know. Ignorance is bliss, in a way, but then... people will take advantage of that ignorance, and might take away the bliss.

I must say the world-building is great; devils exist, but are tied to their name, a feared thing or concept. The greater the fear, the greater the devil's power. As you may imagine, as a reader you will eventually find more than a few interesting devils and fears. And then you have Denji, which goes brrrrm brrrrm while other devils seem to fear him.

Tatsuki Fujimoto's sense of humor and love for "B-movies" is all over the manga (and the anime). A mad genius, if you will. I also read his previous [Fire Punch](https://myanimelist.net/manga/98270) which uses a similar protagonist, but deals with societal themes more than the inner psique of the MC.

However, it's a different manga of his that I hold close to my heart: [Look Back](https://myanimelist.net/manga/138673). It tells the story of two girls who both like drawing and making stories, and decide to become manga authors. It's a heartbreaking short story that got eventually an anime adaptation (which also made me cry), and a live-action adaptation, which apparently got a X-minute standing ovation at the YYY festival. It's a single volume, go read it, or watch it. Bring tissues. He also wrote [Goodbye Eri](https://myanimelist.net/manga/145863), another short one-shot in the same register.

#### [Blue Period](https://myanimelist.net/manga/107931) (ブルーピリオド, _Burū Piriodo_)

![Blue Period volume 1 cover](https://cdn.myanimelist.net/images/manga/2/204827.jpg)

Blue Period follows Yatora Yaguchi, a high school student who is great at everything he tries, has good grades and a solid group of friends, and yet feels completely empty inside — until he stumbles into an art class and finds something that actually makes him feel alive. From there it's about him throwing himself, way too late in the eyes of everyone around him, into getting into the Tokyo University of the Arts, one of the hardest exams there is.

What I love about it is how honest it is about practice versus talent, and about why art even matters in the first place. It doesn't romanticize the starving artist, and it doesn't pretend hard work alone is enough; it sits in that uncomfortable middle. It's by Tsubasa Yamaguchi, who also adapted Makoto Shinkai's [She and Her Cat](https://myanimelist.net/manga/96334). It hits extra close to home because my sister is an artist — I read most of it while she was in uni, and a lot of the fears and frustrations it paints felt like reading about someone I know.

#### [The Fragrant Flower Blooms with Dignity](https://myanimelist.net/manga/144267) ([薫](-かお)る[花](-はな)は[凛](-りん)と[咲](-さ)く, _Kaoru Hana wa Rin to Saku_)

![The Fragrant Flower Blooms with Dignity volume 1 cover](https://cdn.myanimelist.net/images/manga/5/256607.jpg)

The Fragrant Flower Blooms with Dignity is a romance between Rintaro, a big, scary-looking guy from the "delinquent" boys' school, and Kaoruko, a small girl from the elite girls' school next door — a Romeo and Juliet setup that never actually leans into the tragedy. Instead, it's the safest, warmest manga I've ever read. Nothing bad ever really happens; every conflict gets resolved by people actually talking to each other, apologizing, and trying to understand the other side.

Problems are never left to fester for twenty chapters of misunderstanding, and that's exactly why it feels so good. It's by Saka Mikami, and honestly it reads like a blanket — just fuzzy feelings from start to finish.

#### Death Note, Bakuman, and Platinum End

The trio behind these is writer Tsugumi Ohba and artist Takeshi Obata, because I read all three of their big works.

[Death Note](https://myanimelist.net/manga/21) (デスノート, _Desu Nōto_) is the one everyone knows: a notebook that kills whoever's name is written in it, and a cat-and-mouse game between a genius student and a genius detective.

![Death Note volume 1 cover](https://cdn.myanimelist.net/images/manga/1/258245.jpg)

Then I fell in love with [Bakuman](https://myanimelist.net/manga/9711) (バクマン。, _Bakuman._), a slightly sexist (I'll admit) love letter to the world of manga creation — two kids trying to get a series serialized in Shonen Jump. It's one of the things that pushed me to start [drawing](/hobbies/drawing), even if I'm not great at it. Obata also did [Hikaru no Go](https://myanimelist.net/manga/20), if you want his art on a classic.

![Bakuman volume 1 cover](https://cdn.myanimelist.net/images/manga/1/208974.jpg)

Their last one, [Platinum End](https://myanimelist.net/manga/93180) (プラチナエンド, _Purachina Endo_), I did read, but I can't really recommend it — partly the topic, but mostly the... let's call them "dangerous" answers it gives to the questions it raises.

![Platinum End volume 1 cover](https://cdn.myanimelist.net/images/manga/1/172090.jpg)

#### [After God](https://myanimelist.net/manga/143991) (アフターゴッド, _Afutā Goddo_)

![After God volume 1 cover](https://cdn.myanimelist.net/images/manga/1/255516.jpg)

A more recent read and still kinda short. After God opens with Japan having been "invaded" by gods, and it's full of twists and high-speed turns from page one — for the longest time I genuinely couldn't tell who the main character even was, and the story keeps pulling that kind of rug-pull on you again and again.

What got me is how it explores faith: why people worship gods, whether a god is still a god if you can kill it, and what happens to belief when the divine shows up and turns out to be terrifying. It's by Sumi Eno, who also wrote [Ajuutan](https://myanimelist.net/manga/103209), and it's got a rawness to it I really like.

#### [Jujutsu Kaisen](https://myanimelist.net/manga/113138) ([呪術](-じゅじゅつ)[廻戦](-かいせん), _Jujutsu Kaisen_) and its sequel

![Jujutsu Kaisen volume 1 cover](https://cdn.myanimelist.net/images/manga/3/210341.jpg)

At its core, Jujutsu Kaisen is about the burden adult society puts on children and growing young adults — the jujutsu world runs on throwing teenagers at impossible problems while an older, deeply conservative establishment hoards power. It's tradition versus individualism, collectivism versus the self, and a lot of very Japan-specific tension wrapped in great action. It's by Gege Akutami, who also wrote the [Jujutsu Kaisen 0](https://myanimelist.net/manga/115710) prequel (originally Tokyo Metropolitan Curse Technical School).

Its sequel, [Jujutsu Kaisen Modulo](https://myanimelist.net/manga/186597) ([呪術](-じゅじゅつ)[廻戦](-かいせん)≡, _Jujutsu Kaisen Mojuro_) — a short three-volume spin-off set decades later, drawn by Yuuji Iwasaki — goes even harder into current issues: nationalism, racism, and anti-immigration sentiment, with the newly-arrived "aliens" standing in as the latest group Japan has to figure out how (or whether) to coexist with.

![Jujutsu Kaisen Modulo volume 1 cover](https://cdn.myanimelist.net/images/manga/2/339595.jpg)

#### [Domestic Girlfriend](https://myanimelist.net/manga/70941) (ドメスティックな[彼女](-かのじょ), _Domestic na Kanojo_)

![Domestic Girlfriend volume 1 cover](https://cdn.myanimelist.net/images/manga/1/272186.jpg)

Domestic Girlfriend is, on paper, a trashy soap opera — the premise alone (a guy ends up living with his new step-sisters, one of whom is his teacher, the other his recent crush) is absurd. But the surprise is how adult and honest it gets about the characters themselves. The events get ridiculous, but the way people react to them stays mostly real: people make bad decisions, hurt each other, and take years to figure themselves out.

It's by Kei Sasuga, and the character development is genuinely incredible. She also wrote a later series, [Issho ni Kurashite Ii desu ka](https://myanimelist.net/manga/156169/Issho_ni_Kurashite_Ii_desu_ka), that dials the ecchi way down and just focuses on human relationships — because that's always been the interesting part. Her earlier [Good Ending](https://myanimelist.net/manga/15578) (_GE_) is worth a look too.

#### [I Have a Secret](https://myanimelist.net/manga/116734) (かくしごと, _Kakushigoto_)

![I Have a Secret volume 1 cover](https://cdn.myanimelist.net/images/manga/1/219888.jpg)

Kakushigoto is a weird one. A group of classmates each have some kind of odd ability, but the abilities are really just different ways of seeing the world — different frameworks different personalities use to simplify reality into something manageable. And again and again you watch those frameworks crumble when they run into some "unexpected input" the character can't process without actually slowing down, caring, and really listening to people.

It's written by Yoru Sumino (best known for [I Want to Eat Your Pancreas](https://myanimelist.net/manga/104538)) with art by Zui Nieki, and it's short enough to binge in a sitting.

#### Some manhwa

I also read a bunch of manhwa, which are Korean comics — so no Japanese titles here, but they deserve a mention.

[Omniscient Reader's Viewpoint](https://myanimelist.net/manga/132214) (전지적 독자 시점, _Jeonjijeok Dokja Sijeom_) is the best example I've ever seen of taking deus ex machina and making it the entire driving force of the story — and executing that idea flawlessly.

![Omniscient Reader's Viewpoint volume 1 cover](https://cdn.myanimelist.net/images/manga/2/238873.jpg)

[SSS-Class Revival Hunter](https://myanimelist.net/manga/146909) (SSS급 죽어야 사는 헌터, _SSS-geup Jugeoya Saneun Heonteo_) starts from a weird initial concept and turns into a meta-study of storytelling itself.

![SSS-Class Revival Hunter volume 1 cover](https://cdn.myanimelist.net/images/manga/1/263446.jpg)

There are plenty more I won't go deep into: [Your Throne](https://myanimelist.net/manga/130832) (하루만 네가 되고 싶어), [Tower of God](https://myanimelist.net/manga/122663) (신의 탑), [Dice](https://myanimelist.net/manga/147443) (다이스), and on and on.
