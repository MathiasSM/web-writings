![Photo of books](https://placehold.co/600x400){.full}

Truth be told, I used to read more, years ago. It's been a while since I picked up a novel and started reading it without getting distracted or interrupted by life events. I remember it all started with Harry Potter (even if right now I try to avoid anything related to it...), Narnia, some "young adult" books and a failed attempt to go through The Lord of the Rings in Spanish... It may have been the specific translation, but when I compared it to the English version, the latter was way easier to read for me. I managed to finish the first book but I decided not to go on at that time.

Nowadays I tend to read manga, more than anything. As in **a lot of manga**. As of writing and according to my [MyAnimeList](https://myanimelist.net/profile/mathiassm) profile, I have completed 209 series (535 volumes), and I'm currently reading 358 (which is of course an exaggeration, as I basically mark anything I start as "currently reading" and only sometimes do I actually mark it as dropped if I'm sure I'm never finishing it). May sound lame, but I'm a bit proud of my ratings having a somewhat normal distribution centered at only 5.8, as most MAL users give very positively-skewed scores.

Nobody asked, but I'll include here some recommendations...

Now, to start, regardless of who you are, what you do, what you like, where you live, ... Read [One Piece](#one-piece). If you can only choose one thing to read, pick that.

One Piece ([ワンピース]{lang=ja}) {#one-piece}
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

![*One Piece*, vol. 1, © Eiichiro Oda / Shueisha.](https://cdn.myanimelist.net/images/manga/2/253146.jpg)

Eiichiro Oda's ([[尾田](-おだ)[栄一郎](-えいいちろう)]{lang=ja} [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eiichiro_Oda)) _magnus opus_. A story of pirates in the high seas. A story of adventure, freedom and dreams. A story of tyranny, corruption and history. A story as big as its themes, its world, its characters, its heart.

It's longer than most manga at over 1,000 chapters. It's not yet finished and many get intimidated by the sheer length, but I don't know a single person who doesn't love it once they start. I didn't even start reading it until 2025, after many failed attempts at the anime (dropping off _Alabasta_ twice, because of pacing issues of that arc's adaptation). Right now I'm caught up, and it's 100% the best thing I've been reading overall.

It doesn't get good after 300 chapters. You just need to read 12 chapters to meet Chouchou. that was enough for me to get forever hooked.

Some manga authors I follow
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

### Tatsuki Fujimoto ([[藤本](-ふじもと)タツキ]{lang=ja})

<small>[Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatsuki_Fujimoto)</small>

**[Chainsaw Man](https://myanimelist.net/manga/116778) ([チェンソーマン]{lang=ja})**. Assuming you know nothing about it, I wonder what you might imagine it is about. We follow Denji, a guy that has no family, no friends, no money, only his pet dog Pochita, and the incredibly dangerous job of devil hunter, under an abusive boss who only manipulates Denji as long as he's useful. Oh, and Pochita is the chainsaw devil. And because of _reasons_ Denji gains the power to turn into said chainsaw devil... Only to fall into a (slightly better-paid) devil-hunting job.

![*Chainsaw Man*, vol. 1, © Tatsuki Fujimoto / Shueisha.](https://cdn.myanimelist.net/images/manga/3/216464.jpg)

It's about a guy that never went to school, never socialized, doesn't know what's normal, doesn't know what's common or expected. He, however, is for the first time given time (or in some way, permission) to think about his life and his future. He's trying to figure out what he wants, what his dreams are, if he even has any, if he even needs to have any. Fujimoto-sensei portrays him sometimes as a blank whiteboard that the reader can fill with their own desires, goals and morals... Only to then slap you in the face and remind you in his own comical, bloody and grotesque way how your own desires, goals and morals are built _by_ you, or _for_ you, and that it should be you, and only you, who should have the choice to follow them.

Going back to "chansaw goes _brrrm brrrm_", Fujimoto-sensei's sense of humor and love for "B-movies" is all over this and all his other works. From random explosions to borderline absurd solutions, he often uses this to mask deeper themes and ideas (although I'm pretty sure that _Sharknado_ reference was just him having fun). His previous **[Fire Punch](https://myanimelist.net/manga/98270)** is set in a postapocalyptic world, and explores themes of religious fanatism, collective delusion, death, through the eyes of a main character who's in constant agony, _literally being burned alive for years_. But that doesn't stop him from adding a very important character that loves movies and wants to make one, in a world where movies have ceased to exist... Or rather, "movies" have been perverted into being simply tools for propaganda and indoctrination. Movies are portrayed, in the author's unique way, as the ultimate artifact of human emotion; such is the importance cinema as a whole is given in almost every one of Fujimoto-sensei's stories.

If that wasn't enough for you, he also wrote **[Goodbye Eri](https://myanimelist.net/manga/145863)**, a short one-shot about a young boy's grief, through the lenses of his smartphone, as he films the last days of the terminally ill Eri. The manga panels are all (most?) horizontal and made to be literally what the phone is capturing. It plays tricks on the reader in many ways, as you never know if what you're reading is what he's filming, and whether what he's filming is the true Eri, a fictional version of her, or something else entirely. 

However, it's a different manga of his that I hold close to my heart. One that doesn't contain obvious portrayals of cinema or movies, but that it's clearly written like one, paced like one, even if not drawn like one the way Goodbye Eri is. **[Look Back](https://myanimelist.net/manga/138673)** tells the story of two girls who like drawing and making stories, and who soon decide to become manga authors. It's a short story about frustration, isolation, grief and healing. It constantly asks the question of why we create art, and it never stops to answer, or explain. It simply shows you: Even when it is hard, or frustrating, or takes a long time, even if it requires you to give up other parts of yourself, even _then_ you can find worth in what you did, you just need to remember the title of this story. 

It eventually got an anime adaptation (which also made me cry), and a live-action adaptation, which apparently got a 16-minute standing ovation at the Venice Film Festival along with 5 awards. So yeah, I can't wait to watch it someday. In the meantime, I might re-read the manga, again. It's a single volume, go read it, or watch the animated movie adaptation. Either way bring tissues.

### Tsugumi Ohba ([[大場](-おおば)つぐみ]{lang=ja}) and Takeshi Obata ([[小畑](-おばた)[健](-たけし)]{lang=ja})

 <small>[Tsugumi Ohba's Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsugumi_Ohba) · [Takeshi Obata's Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takeshi_Obata)</small>

**[Death Note](https://myanimelist.net/manga/21) ([デスノート]{lang=ja})** is the one everyone knows: a notebook that kills whoever's name is written in it, and a cat-and-mouse game between a genius student and a genius detective. Incredibly popular, even if slightly outdated. It's the classic "justified evil" story, with all its tropes, but with a excellent execution. I can recommend it even to non-anime viewers, and even to people who don't like fantasy elements, as they are very _well_ constrained in this story (with a literal in-universe written ruleset).

Some time after (I was still in high-school), I fell in love with **[Bakuman](https://myanimelist.net/manga/9711) ([バクマン。]{lang=ja})**, a (I accept to be) slightly sexist love letter to the world of manga creation: Two kids trying to get a series serialized in Shonen Jump (the biggest and probably most important manga publication today). It's one of the things that pushed me to start drawing for fun, even if I'm not great at it.

### Gege Akutami ([芥見](-あくたみ)[下々](-げげ))

<small>[Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gege_Akutami)</small>

**[Jujutsu Kaisen](https://myanimelist.net/manga/113138) ([[呪術](-じゅじゅつ)[廻戦](-かいせん)]{lang=ja})** is at its core about the burden adult society puts on children and growing young adults - the jujutsu world runs on throwing teenagers at impossible problems while an older, deeply conservative establishment hoards power. It's tradition versus individualism, collectivism versus the self, and a lot of very Japan-specific tension wrapped in great action. He also wrote the [Jujutsu Kaisen 0](https://myanimelist.net/manga/115710) prequel (originally Tokyo Metropolitan Curse Technical School).

::::: figures

![*Jujutsu Kaisen*, vol. 1, © Gege Akutami / Shueisha.](https://cdn.myanimelist.net/images/manga/3/210341.jpg)

![*Jujutsu Kaisen Modulo*, vol. 1, © Gege Akutami & Yuuji Iwasaki / Shueisha.](https://cdn.myanimelist.net/images/manga/2/339595.jpg)

:::::

Its sequel, **[Jujutsu Kaisen Modulo](https://myanimelist.net/manga/186597) ([[呪術](-じゅじゅつ)[廻戦](-かいせん)≡]{lang=ja})** - a short three-volume spin-off set decades later, drawn by Yuuji Iwasaki ([[岩崎](-いわさき)[優次](-ゆうじ)]{lang=ja} <small>[Wikipedia](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%B2%A9%E5%B4%8E%E5%84%AA%E6%AC%A1) · [MyAnimeList](https://myanimelist.net/people/48257/Yuuji_Iwasaki)</small>) - goes even harder into current issues: nationalism, racism, and anti-immigration sentiment, with the newly-arrived "aliens" standing in as the latest group Japan has to figure out how (or whether) to coexist with.

### Hiromu Arakawa ([[荒川](-あらかわ)[弘](-ひろむ)]{lang=ja})

<small>[Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiromu_Arakawa)</small>

**[Fullmetal Alchemist](https://myanimelist.net/manga/25) ([[鋼](-はがね)の[錬金術師](-れんきんじゅつし)]{lang=ja}, _Hagane no Renkinjutsushi_)** - maybe the best-known one here. Follow the story of two brothers in a world where "Alchemy" is practiced as a science (although it works as the magic system in the story). After a traumatic event during their childhood, they are on a self-imposed mission to recover what they lost.

![*Fullmetal Alchemist*, vol. 1, © Hiromu Arakawa / Square Enix.](https://cdn.myanimelist.net/images/manga/3/243675.jpg)

The main theme is (in my opinion), the question: "What is the value of a human life?". From that, many other questions arise within the grounded world Arakawa builds. Trauma, family, religion, politics, good vs evil. It may simplify some of these topics, of course, but I still think they are handled very well.

The fact that the magic system is based on knowledge and scientific training is also a plus.

Arakawa also wrote **[Silver Spoon](https://myanimelist.net/manga/25096) (_Gin no Saji_)**, a far more chill story about an agricultural school, if you want proof of how wide her range is.

### Kei Sasuga ([[流石](-さすが)[景](-けい)]{lang=ja})

<small>[Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kei_Sasuga)</small>

**[Domestic Girlfriend](https://myanimelist.net/manga/70941) ([ドメスティックな[彼女](-かのじょ)]{lang=ja}, _Domestic na Kanojo_)** is, on paper, a trashy soap opera - the premise alone (a guy ends up living with his new step-sisters, one of whom is his teacher, the other his recent crush) is absurd. But the surprise is how adult and honest it gets about the characters themselves. The events get ridiculous, but the way people react to them stays mostly real: people make bad decisions, hurt each other, and take years to figure themselves out.

![*Domestic Girlfriend*, vol. 1, © Kei Sasuga / Kodansha.](https://cdn.myanimelist.net/images/manga/1/272186.jpg)

The character development is genuinely incredible. She also wrote a later series, **[Issho ni Kurashite Ii desu ka](https://myanimelist.net/manga/156169/Issho_ni_Kurashite_Ii_desu_ka)**, that dials the ecchi way down and just focuses on human relationships - because that's always been the interesting part. Her earlier **[Good Ending](https://myanimelist.net/manga/15578) (_GE_)** is worth a look too.

### Tsubasa Yamaguchi ([[山口](-やまぐち)つばさ]{lang=ja})

<small>[Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsubasa_Yamaguchi)</small>

**[Blue Period](https://myanimelist.net/manga/107931) ([ブルーピリオド]{lang=ja})** follows Yatora Yaguchi, a high school student who is great at everything he tries, has good grades and a solid group of friends, and yet feels completely empty inside - until he stumbles into an art class and finds something that actually makes him feel alive. From there it's about him throwing himself, way too late in the eyes of everyone around him, into getting into the Tokyo University of the Arts, one of the hardest exams there is.

![*Blue Period*, vol. 1, © Tsubasa Yamaguchi / Kodansha.](https://cdn.myanimelist.net/images/manga/2/204827.jpg)

What I love about it is how honest it is about practice versus talent, and about why art even matters in the first place. It doesn't romanticize the starving artist, and it doesn't pretend hard work alone is enough; it sits in that uncomfortable middle. She also adapted Makoto Shinkai's [She and Her Cat](https://myanimelist.net/manga/96334). It hits extra close to home because my sister is an artist - I read most of it while she was in uni, and a lot of the fears and frustrations it paints felt like reading about someone I know.

### Yoru Sumino ([[住野](-すみの)よる]{lang=ja})

<small>[Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoru_Sumino)</small>

![*I Have a Secret*, vol. 1, © Yoru Sumino & Zui Nieki / Shinchosha.](https://cdn.myanimelist.net/images/manga/1/219888.jpg)

**[I Have a Secret](https://myanimelist.net/manga/116734) ([かくしごと]{lang=ja}, _Kakushigoto_)** is a weird one. A group of classmates each have some kind of odd ability, but the abilities are really just different ways of seeing the world - different frameworks that different personalities use to simplify reality into something manageable. And again and again you watch those frameworks crumble when they run into some "unexpected input" the character can't process without actually slowing down, caring, and really listening to people.

Sumino is best known for **[I Want to Eat Your Pancreas](https://myanimelist.net/manga/104538)**; the art here is by Zui Nieki ([[二駅](-にえき)ずい]{lang=ja}), and it's short enough to binge in a sitting.

### Attack on Titan ([[進撃](-しんげき)の[巨人](-きょじん)]{lang=ja})

![*Attack on Titan*, vol. 1, © Hajime Isayama / Kodansha.](https://cdn.myanimelist.net/images/manga/2/37846.jpg)

More recent, and some would say more modern. In **[Attack on Titan](https://myanimelist.net/manga/23390)** Humanity has been reduced and secluded within tall walls to flee from the titans, creatures with no intellect but a constant unrelenting drive to eat said humans. After 100 years of peace within the walls, which are taller than those titans by a long shot, a _colossal_ titan appears, looking over the wall as if it weren't 50 meters tall, kicks a hole into it, and disappears, letting in a horde of titans. The story will follow a few cadets who later join the anti-titan military force, with the impossible goal of eradicating them, and freeing humanity.

It's a tricky entry, because beyond a surface of action and gore, it deals with darker themes of politics, history, fascism, genocide, wrapped in the fantasy mystery of the titans. I enjoyed the foreshadowing, the mystery, the incredible revelations every once in a while (most readers remember the exact chapter number or name where certain questions were answered). The author started the manga pretending it was the usual action-packed fantasy nonsense, only to uncover an increasingly complicated world little by little, increasing tension and worries everywhere.

It's not perfect; in particular, certain "answers" given at the very end were _controversial_ to say the least (especially looking at the real world right now...). You don't have to agree with the answers given (the author himself doesn't even seem to agree, either, from what I've read), but the fact it made people question and discuss these topics is worth a lot, I feel.

It's by Hajime Isayama ([[諫山](-いさやま)[創](-はじめ)]{lang=ja}), and as far as I know he hasn't really done anything else major - this is his whole thing, which honestly makes the consistency of the mystery even more impressive.

### The Fragrant Flower Blooms with Dignity ([[薫](-かお)る[花](-はな)は[凛](-りん)と[咲](-さ)く]{lang=ja})

![*The Fragrant Flower Blooms with Dignity*, vol. 1, © Saka Mikami / Kodansha.](https://cdn.myanimelist.net/images/manga/5/256607.jpg)

**[The Fragrant Flower Blooms with Dignity](https://myanimelist.net/manga/143991)** is a romance between Rintaro, a big, scary-looking guy from the "delinquent" boys' school, and Kaoruko, a small girl from the elite girls' school next door - a Romeo and Juliet setup that never actually leans into the tragedy. Instead, it's the safest, warmest manga I've ever read. Nothing bad ever really happens; every conflict gets resolved by people actually talking to each other, apologizing, and trying to understand the other side.

Problems are never left to fester for twenty chapters of misunderstanding, and that's exactly why it feels so good. It's by Saka Mikami ([[三香見](-みかみ)サカ]{lang=ja}), and honestly it reads like a blanket - just fuzzy feelings from start to finish.

### After God ([アフターゴッド]{lang=ja})

![*After God*, vol. 1, © Sumi Eno / Shogakukan.](https://cdn.myanimelist.net/images/manga/1/255516.jpg)

A more recent read and still kinda short. **[After God](https://myanimelist.net/manga/143991)** opens with Japan having been "invaded" by gods, and it's full of twists and high-speed turns from page one - for the longest time I genuinely couldn't tell who the main character even was, and the story keeps pulling that kind of rug-pull on you again and again.

What got me is how it explores faith: why people worship gods, whether a god is still a god if you can kill it, and what happens to belief when the divine shows up and turns out to be terrifying. It's by Sumi Eno ([[江野](-えの)[朱美](-すみ)]{lang=ja}), who also wrote **[Ajuutan](https://myanimelist.net/manga/103209)**, and it's got a rawness to it I really like.

About manhwa, the Korean variety of comics
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

::::: figures

![*Omniscient Reader's Viewpoint*, vol. 1, © singNsong & Sleepy-C / Naver Webtoon.](https://cdn.myanimelist.net/images/manga/2/238873.jpg)

![*SSS-Class Revival Hunter*, vol. 1, © Sinnoa & Bill K / KakaoPage.](https://cdn.myanimelist.net/images/manga/1/263446.jpg)

:::::

[Omniscient Reader's Viewpoint](https://myanimelist.net/manga/132214) ([전지적 독자 시점]{lang=ko}) is the best example I've ever seen of taking _deus ex machina_ and making it the entire driving force of the story, and yet executing that idea flawlessly.


[SSS-Class Revival Hunter](https://myanimelist.net/manga/146909) ([SSS급 죽어야 사는 헌터]{lang=ko}) starts from a weird initial concept and turns into a meta-study of storytelling itself.

There are plenty more I won't go deep into: [Your Throne](https://myanimelist.net/manga/130832) ([하루만 네가 되고 싶어]{lang=ko}), [Tower of God](https://myanimelist.net/manga/122663) ([신의 탑]{lang=ko}), [Dice](https://myanimelist.net/manga/147443) ([다이스]{lang=ko}), and on and on.
